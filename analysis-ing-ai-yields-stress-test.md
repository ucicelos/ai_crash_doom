# Stress-testing ING's claim: AI accounts for ~20% of the rise in long-dated yields

**Published:** October 4, 2026
**Source note:** [ING THINK, "To what extent is the rise in long-dated rates attributable to AI?"](https://think.ing.com/articles/to-what-extent-is-the-rise-in-long-dated-rates-attributable-to-ai/)

## 1. What ING actually claims

ING's rates-strategy note offers a **"suggested" breakdown** of the rise in long-dated rates (10-year Treasury ≈ 5.2% at the time of writing):

| Driver | Share |
|---|---|
| Inflation profile | 50% |
| Fiscal deficit *narrative* | 30% |
| AI piece | 20% |

And within the AI piece:

| AI sub-channel | Share of the 20% | = Share of total |
|---|---|---|
| Spending (GDP growth accounting) | 5% | ~1 pt |
| Capital-markets issuance | 25% | ~5 pts |
| Productivity | 70% | ~14 pts |

The note is explicit that this is a heuristic, not an econometric decomposition. That honesty is its best feature — and the reason the headline number needs stress-testing before it goes on a dashboard.

## 2. What holds up: the issuance channel (~5 pts of the 20)

This is the defensible core, and it's the part ING supports with real numbers:

- **Year-to-date USD corporate issuance hit $878bn**, exceeding *full-year* issuance in every year from 2021–2024, and running **54% above the same period last year**. The increase is driven by TMT, **tripling to $330bn**.
- At the long end specifically: **corporate long-dated issuance up $55bn vs. last year**, while long-end Treasury buybacks rose $20bn — a **$75bn net swing** in long-end supply pressure *toward corporates, away from Treasuries*.
- AI-spend issuers chose bonds over bank loans (banks' funding is too short to match), so the long end absorbs it.
- **New-issue premiums on long/AI-related paper are ~10bp** — "far from tipping the long end over the edge," per ING, but directionally pressing.

Sanity check: the 10-year rose roughly 100bp from its February 2026 low (~4.3%) to ~5.28–5.30%. Twenty percent of that ≈ 20bp. A $75bn long-end supply swing plausibly explains a low-single-to-low-double-digit number of basis points of that — **the issuance channel is consistent with roughly 5–10 of ING's 20 points**. This part survives.

## 3. What doesn't: the productivity channel (~14 pts of the 20)

Seventy percent of ING's AI number — the single largest slice of the entire AI claim — rests on what the note itself calls **"more mythical in nature"** reasoning:

> *Theoretical chain: higher productivity → higher expected return on capital → higher investment spending financed through higher issuance → higher real rates.*

Problems:

- **No empirical decomposition.** The note offers no attribution of the observed real-yield rise (10-year TIPS went from ~1.7% in 2024 to ~2.91%) to AI productivity versus Fed-policy expectations, term-premium normalization, or the end of QE. The 14 points are asserted, not derived.
- **Comparison to 2000 is suggestive, not probative.** ING notes the 10-year real yield was 4% in 2000 (nominal >6%) and is now 2.9% (nominal 5.2%), concluding "we're still at least 100bp below the levels we saw back then." That reads as a ceiling argument, not a decomposition.
- **The circularity that matters most:** the productivity story is the *bull case*. If AI productivity is real, higher real yields are "for positive reasons" and there's headroom to 4% real — the boom can run. The bear case needs productivity gains to **not** materialize while issuance keeps coming. ING assigns 70% of the AI weight to the component that argues *against* a rates-driven collapse.
- **The spending piece (~1 pt)** is internally hedged by ING itself: "AI accounts for a third of current economic growth" sits next to the admission that the AI piece is "more of a filler than anything else" given the non-AI economy is less dynamic.

## 4. Cross-check against independent decompositions

- **TD Economics** decomposed the ~80bp rise from the February low to early September: **50bp from expected short-term rates, 20bp from term premium, 10bp from inflation expectations.** ([source](https://www.ainvest.com/news/term-premium-fed-carrying-higher-longer-2609/)) ING's AI-issuance channel would live mostly inside that 20bp term-premium slice — consistent with a 5–10bp contribution, not 20+.
- **ACM term premium** sits around +100–136bp, near decade highs — the long end is being priced on compensation, not Fed expectations. That's fertile ground for a supply-driven story, but it doesn't isolate AI supply from Treasury supply or the fiscal narrative.
- **Q3 2026 alone: the 10-year rose 87bp**, the largest quarterly increase since Q1 1994. Friday's soft NFP (29k, unemployment 4.2%) couldn't reverse it — "NFP won the first move; inflation risk won the closing argument." The market is pricing a regime, not a data point.

## 5. Verdict

**The headline "20%" is a suggested heuristic, not a measured fact — but roughly half of it is solid.**

- **Defensible (5–10 points):** the AI/TMT corporate issuance boom is real, measurable ($878bn YTD, TMT $330bn, $75bn long-end swing), and plausibly adds ~5–10bp of pressure to long yields. This is the part worth citing.
- **Faith-based (~14 points):** the productivity channel is an article of belief that ING itself flags as "mythical." It cannot be verified from the note, and — critically — it's the *optimistic* component: it says AI deserves higher real yields.
- **Misleading if quoted baldly:** "AI accounts for 20% of the yield rise" sounds bearish. The honest restatement is: **AI-linked corporate supply accounts for maybe a tenth of the yield rise; the other half of ING's number is the claim that AI productivity justifies higher real yields — which, if true, extends the boom rather than ending it.**

## 6. What this means for the collapse thesis

The issuance channel *strengthens* the credit-compression thesis: AI capex financed in the bond market is adding duration supply at the long end while the fiscal deficit keeps Treasury supply elevated — both push term premium up, and Paramount's record $52bn debt sale at up to 9.1% this week is the lived version of that mechanism.

But the stress test cuts one way that matters: **if the productivity piece of ING's number is right, real yields have another 100bp of "justified" headroom before hitting 2000 levels.** The disorderly-break case needs AI to deliver the issuance without delivering the productivity. Watch realized productivity data (TFP prints, AI revenue realization) as the tiebreaker — not just the yield chart.
